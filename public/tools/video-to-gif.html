Video to GIF

A short clip turned into an animated GIF, with a hand-written encoder.

Create a short silent looping animation from a video segment. GIF is convenient for demonstrations but usually much larger than a compressed video of the same scene.

How to use this tool

  1. Open a video and select a short section.
  2. Set a modest width and frame rate.
  3. Create the GIF and inspect the loop before downloading.

Worked example

A three-second interface demonstration at 360 pixels wide and 10 fps produces about 30 frames. Cropping the subject before conversion can reduce the necessary image area.

Limitations and checks

GIF has no audio and limited colors. The selected segment is capped at 15 seconds. Long, high-resolution sequences can use substantial memory and create huge files. Use video when the destination supports it.

Read the related workflow guide for choosing settings and verifying exports.

How not to end up with a 20 MB GIF

GIF dates from 1987 and allows only 256 colours per image, so the size runs away fast. Three rules: under six seconds, 360 px wide or less, and 10 frames per second. That keeps a typical clip between 1 and 3 MB.

The encoder here - palette, LZW compression and block framing - is written from scratch, with no library downloaded to do it.
