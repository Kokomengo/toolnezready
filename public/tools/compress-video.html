Compress video

Drop the resolution and bitrate until it fits wherever it needs to go.

Reduce a video for a file-size limit by choosing bitrate and resolution. Duration, motion and audio also affect the final size; the estimate is a planning aid rather than a promise.

How to use this tool

  1. Select a video and trim unnecessary seconds.
  2. Choose a width and bitrate suitable for the content; optionally remove audio.
  3. Compress with the tab active, then verify the output size and playback.

Video is re-encoded by playing it through, so the job takes roughly as long as the selected clip. Keep the tab visible while it runs.

Worked example

At 1 Mbps, 30 seconds of video uses roughly 3.75 MB before audio and container overhead. Use a higher bitrate if text or fast motion becomes blocky.

Limitations and checks

Output may be larger than an already efficient source. Audio increases the size beyond the video-only estimate. Browser support determines the actual output format.

Read the related workflow guide for choosing settings and verifying exports.

Which setting to touch first

Trim unnecessary seconds first, then choose resolution and bitrate together. Lower resolution can reduce detail; final size also depends on bitrate, duration and audio. Try 1 Mbps for a short talking-head sample, then inspect motion and small text. Fast scenes may need a higher bitrate to avoid blocking.
