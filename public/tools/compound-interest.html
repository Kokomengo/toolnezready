Compound interest

What happens if you put the same amount in every month for years.

Model a starting amount with regular end-of-month contributions and a constant nominal annual return divided into monthly periods. Use scenarios to understand assumptions rather than predict an investment.

How to use this tool

  1. Enter initial savings, monthly contribution, annual return and years.
  2. Review the paid-in amount separately from modeled growth.
  3. Try lower-return scenarios and account for inflation, taxes and fees separately.
—

Worked example

With 100 initially, no contributions and 12% nominal annual return, twelve monthly periods at 1% produce about 112.68. Contributions in this tool are added after each month’s growth.

Limitations and checks

Returns are not guaranteed and losses are possible. Inflation, fees and taxes are excluded. This is a fixed-rate illustration; it does not model market volatility or personalized financial advice.

The detail almost everyone forgets

These figures are nominal: they ignore inflation and capital gains tax. If you expect 6% a year with inflation at 2%, the annual real-rate approximation is (1.06 / 1.02) - 1, or about 3.92%. This tool uses a nominal annual rate divided into monthly periods; it does not model inflation-adjusted contributions, taxes, fees or changing market returns.
