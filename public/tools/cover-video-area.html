Cover an area in a video

Blur, pixelate or black out a rectangle for the whole clip.

Apply a fixed blur, pixelation or solid rectangle to a video you own. The rectangle remains at the same screen position throughout the processed clip.

How to use this tool

  1. Load the video and draw a rectangle over the area.
  2. Choose blur, pixelation or a solid cover.
  3. Process the video and watch the whole result before sharing.

Use this on your own images and videos. Removing another author's signature or mark in order to reuse their work is copyright infringement, and in many countries an offence as well.

Worked example

A solid rectangle can cover a stationary name label. If a face moves outside that rectangle, this tool will not follow it: use tracking in a dedicated editor.

Limitations and checks

There is no automatic motion tracking. Blur and pixelation may leave information recognizable; a solid cover is more suitable for readable private text. Check every scene and audio separately.

Read the related workflow guide for choosing settings and verifying exports.

Three ways to cover something

Blur hides without drawing attention and is the usual choice for faces or addresses. Pixelate is the classic television effect: more noticeable, but it makes clear that something was deliberately hidden. Black out is the safest option when the information must not leak under any circumstances, because the other two, at very low strength, are sometimes reversible.

Common uses

  • Covering the faces of people who did not agree to appear before you publish the video.
  • Hiding number plates, addresses, account numbers or names in a screen recording.
  • Removing the logo or timecode your own camera or software burns in.
  • Covering a notification that popped up in the middle of a demo.

The rectangle is stored as proportions rather than pixels, so you can change the output resolution and the covered area stays where it should.

Common questions

Does the covered area follow the object?

No, it is a fixed rectangle for the whole segment. If what you want hidden moves a lot, process the video in several segments, each with its own rectangle, and join them afterwards.

Why does it take as long as the video lasts?

Because it is re-encoded by playing it back in real time with MediaRecorder, frame by frame.
